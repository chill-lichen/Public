One example

Put the pieces together once. Stop rebuilding them every month.

Invoice data might be in your accounting system. Hours sit in a time-tracking app. Someone maintains a separate list of projects. A useful monthly report needs a way to bring them together and check the answers.

  • See what's been invoiced, paid, and left outstanding
  • Compare jobs or services when cost information is available
  • Make the report repeatable for the next person

Why me?

I've spent years fixing the reporting behind the report.

At NXT Capital I helped cut a monthly reporting process from five days to one. At BMO I worked on automated executive reporting that reduced manual effort by about 90%. Those were projects in previous jobs, not client claims for this practice.

I also worked in business brokerage. That changed how I look at business reporting: an owner should be able to explain their numbers, not just produce them.
