# V2 website prototype — creative direction and review brief

**Status:** separate prototype. This does **not** replace \`docs/index.html\`. The prototype is available at \`docs/v2/index.html\` when GitHub Pages has deployed the commit. Keep it out of search engines while we evaluate it.

## Positioning

**Audience:** established independent professionals and owner-led small service businesses, particularly those that do not have an in-house analyst or operations reporting function. Not every solopreneur has enough data complexity or budget to warrant a project; do not claim otherwise.

**Business problem:** a growing backlog of spreadsheets, manual reports, and owner-only processes makes it hard to see margins, available cash, commitments, and day-to-day operational dependencies.

**Value proposition:** make business information actionable, and the process for producing it repeatable, through tightly scoped work. Sell a decision-ready outcome, not "digital transformation" or a list of software skills.

**Natural longer-term adjacency:** understanding cash flow, operating metrics, customer concentration, and owner dependency is useful for business-owner financial planning and M&A readiness. Those are adjacent interests—not services or credentials currently advertised.

## Homepage structure

1. Owner-first hero: "Know which work pays. See where cash goes."
2. Fictional owner dashboard: shows cash in bank, open invoices, revenue, cash trend, and revenue mix.
3. Three specific owner questions: margin, hiring/cash, and inability to step away.
4. Before/after process illustration: scattered inputs to one monthly management view.
5. Three testable engagements: Know your numbers; Put reporting on repeat; Make work easier to hand off.
6. About: grounded professional background; explicitly no imaginary firm, client logos or testimonials.
7. Straightforward project approach and one main CTA into the existing Notion form.

## Visual design

- Ink \`#1B2435\` for substantive text and authoritative contrast.
- Cream \`#F5F2EC\` as an editorial, comfortable canvas.
- Blue \`#4269CA\` for interaction and analytical emphasis.
- Clay \`#C17955\` sparingly for warmth and accent.
- Slate \`#697481\` for supporting text.
- Serif display typography with restrained sans-serif UI elements; no externally hosted font files.
- White-space, asymmetry, samples, and layout hierarchy—not generic stock photography or decorative AI imagery.

## Claims and boundaries

- Dashboard figures, line chart and process graphic are clearly illustrative and fictional. They are **not** client results, testimonials, a live subscription product, or a claimed achievable return.
- Avoid titles "CFP", "CPA", "fractional CFO", "M&A advisor", and regulated advisory promises unless separately verified.
- Do not claim licenses, financial planning services, legal/tax advice, broker-dealer services, or formal valuation engagements on this site.
- No public pricing or payment collection until actual capacity, costs, scope and terms are agreed.
- Link to existing Terms and Privacy pages. Notion handles the separate intake form. This preview itself has no Apollo tracker.
- The site is a working personal prototype, not evidence that market demand exists.

## What should be validated before promotion

- Does a real small-business owner recognize their problem from the hero within five seconds?
- Do the three offers feel specific and worth paying for, or too broad?
- Does the dashboard look plausible, useful, and appropriately labeled as sample data?
- Is it clear what a first engagement delivers—and what is **not** included?
- Does a visitor trust the actual individual behind the offer without fabricated proof?
- Do the CTA, Notion intake, and privacy/terms links work on mobile and desktop?
- Does the ink/cream/blue palette fit the owner's taste more than the original green palette?
- Is a genuine photograph available to replace the text-only About block without pretending to represent an established company?

## Next iteration once approved

- Refine copy with reactions from three to five real target business owners.
- Request permission to use authentic photos and eventually anonymized client work samples.
- Consider a distinct "Services" page only if prospects need additional detail after reading the homepage.
- Add pricing examples only after delivering a few scoped projects and learning actual effort.
- Preserve current production homepage and workflows until review is complete.
